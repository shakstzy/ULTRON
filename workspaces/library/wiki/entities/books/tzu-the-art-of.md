## TL;DR

A 2400-year-old field manual that reads like a startup ops doc. Win without fighting when you can. When you must fight, win on the math you ran before you showed up. Speed and deception beat raw force.

## Key takeaways

- The win is decided before the engagement. Whoever calculates more in advance wins more often. "No calculation" is just losing on a delay.
- The best outcome is to break the other side's will without a fight. Sieges and grinding battles are the worst tier of strategy, not heroic ones.
- All warfare is deception. Look weak when strong, look far when near. Public posture and private capability should be opposites.
- Speed beats elegance. Long campaigns drain the state faster than they break the enemy. "Stupid haste" still beats "clever delay."
- Know yourself AND the enemy. One out of two means you trade wins for losses. Zero out of two means you lose every time.
- Centralized planning, decentralized execution. The ruler sets the mission and stays out of the field. The general adapts to what is actually in front of them.

## Quote

> "All warfare is based on deception."
> — Sun Tzu, ch. I, trans. Giles
