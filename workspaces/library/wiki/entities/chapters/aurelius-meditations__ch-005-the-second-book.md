## TL;DR

Written in a war camp at Carnuntum. The opening line is "Remember how long thou hast already put off these things." From there it is a tight loop: time is short, only the present moment is yours, do every action as if it were your last. The whole chapter is Aurelius prodding himself to stop waiting.

## Key takeaways

- The procrastination diagnosis is brutal: you keep deferring becoming who you mean to be, and the gods set a deadline you can't see.
- The only piece of life you can actually lose is the present moment. Past and future aren't yours to give up. So a long life and a short life lose the same thing at the end.
- The "act as if dying" filter cuts vanity, hypocrisy, and self-love out of a decision in one pass.
- External events — death, dishonor, poverty, pleasure — are neither good nor bad. Universal nature lets them fall on good and bad people equally, which proves they are not the point.
- Lust is worse than anger because lust signs up willingly. Anger at least has the excuse of being a flinch from grief.
- Don't measure yourself against other men's souls — measure yourself against your own reason. He says nobody is unhappy from not knowing another's soul; everyone is unhappy from ignoring their own.

## Quote

"The time of a man's life is as a point." — Aurelius, Book II, trans. Long

## Why it matters

This is the chapter to reread at the start of any week that feels both urgent and pointless. It collapses the two anxieties — running out of time, and chasing the wrong things — into one move: do the next action well, now.
