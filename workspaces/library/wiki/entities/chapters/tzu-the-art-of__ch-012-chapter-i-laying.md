## TL;DR

War is a state-survival question, so you run the math in your tent before you ever take the field. Five factors, seven comparisons, and one rule: all warfare is deception.

## Key takeaways

- Five factors that determine who wins: moral alignment with your people, weather and timing, terrain, the commander's character, and discipline / logistics. Anyone running ops without scoring all five is gambling.
- A commander is judged on five virtues — wisdom, sincerity, benevolence, courage, strictness. Missing any one of them is a known failure mode, not a personality quirk.
- Before fighting, score both sides on seven concrete comparisons: whose people are bought in, whose general is better, who has the timing and ground, whose discipline is real, whose force is stronger, whose troops are trained, and whose rewards and punishments are consistent. Honest scoring forecasts the outcome.
- Plans are the starting point, not the script. "Modify one's plans" as circumstances shift — the enemy's moves dictate yours, not your pre-written PRD.
- The full deception playbook: look weak when strong, look idle when moving, look far when near. Bait the greedy, irritate the angry, exhaust the rested, split the unified. Strike where they are not prepared.
- More calculations beforehand wins. Few calculations loses. Zero calculations is automatic loss. Pre-mortems are not optional.

## Quote

> "All warfare is based on deception."
> — Sun Tzu, ch. I, trans. Giles

## Why it matters

This is the planning checklist. Every "we'll figure it out in the room" instinct is a vote against your future self. Score the factors, run the comparisons, then go.
