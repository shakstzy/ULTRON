## TL;DR

The best win is the one where nothing got broken — theirs or yours. Capture intact beats destroy. Break the enemy's plan before you ever swing. Sieges are the worst tier of strategy, not the most heroic.

## Key takeaways

- The hierarchy of attack, best to worst: ruin their plan, sever their alliances, fight their army in open field, besiege their walls. Most people skip straight to the siege because it feels decisive. It is not.
- "Supreme excellence is breaking the enemy's resistance without fighting." If you have to engage, your strategy already failed one tier up.
- Force-ratio doctrine: 10-to-1 surround, 5-to-1 attack, 2-to-1 split into pincer, even match offer battle, slightly under avoid, badly outnumbered flee. Honest math about your numbers is required to even pick the move.
- The general is the load-bearing wall of the state. Gaps in the general's competence become structural weakness everywhere downstream.
- Three ways a ruler ruins their own army: micromanaging troop movement they don't understand (hobbling), running the army like a civilian government (confusion), and putting the wrong people in the wrong roles (loss of trust). Same three failure modes apply to founders running engineering orgs.
- The five conditions for winning: knowing when to fight, knowing how to handle uneven numbers, unity through the ranks, being prepared while the enemy isn't, and being trusted to operate without interference from above.
- The closing rule. Know yourself and the enemy, you don't fear a hundred battles. Know one, you trade wins for losses. Know neither, you lose every time.

## Quote

> "Supreme excellence consists in breaking the enemy's resistance without fighting."
> — Sun Tzu, ch. III, trans. Giles

## Why it matters

This is the chapter Adithya needs to re-read before any negotiation or competitive launch. The instinct is always to engage; the move is usually to dissolve the engagement before it forms. Also: ship founder-autonomy as a constraint, not a perk — a ruler that interferes mid-battle is the named failure mode.
